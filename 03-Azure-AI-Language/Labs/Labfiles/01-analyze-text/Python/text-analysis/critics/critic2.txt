7/10 | Visually stunning, but the third act and audio mixing hold it back
Reviewed by: Cinephile_Dave

There is no denying that Christopher Nolan is a visionary. The practical effects and CGI in Interstellar are completely seamless, and the sequence on Miller's planet (the water world where every hour equals seven years) is one of the most tense, brilliantly executed scenes of the 21st century.

That being said, I have to dock a few points. First, the sound mixing. I appreciate Zimmer's score, but I actually want to hear what the actors are saying without turning on subtitles. Second, the climax. The film spends two hours grounding itself in hard science with the help of Kip Thorne, only to hand-wave the resolution with a monologue about how "love is a quantifiable force that transcends dimensions." It felt a bit clunky and overly sentimental for a movie that was trying so hard to be the next 2001: A Space Odyssey. Still, it's a must-watch for the visuals alone.
